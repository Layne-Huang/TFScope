Figure 1 · TFScope architecture. A frozen ESM-2 (650M) protein language model produces per-residue contextual embeddings; low-rank (LoRA) adapters on the last six transformer layers allow lightweight task adaptation. A learned DBD position indicator marks DNA-binding-domain residues, after which a dual-stream gated attention pool extracts a global TF representation and a DBD-focused representation in parallel — the sigmoidal value gate prevents attention sinks and suppresses uninformative residues. The pooled streams are fused and routed through a family-conditioned Mixture-of-Experts decoder containing twelve SwiGLU experts with top-2 routing, an always-active shared expert, a FiLM modulation pathway, and a 32-slot prototype dictionary that exposes interpretable binding concepts. Family routing is biased by a semantic embedding constructed from ProTrek text and ESM-2 sequence vectors, enabling generalization to unseen TF families. Two heads decode the result at fixed length 20: a position-gate head produces a continuous, ordinal-regularized motif mask, while a PWM regression head combines self-attention across positions with cross-attention back to the DBD residues, yielding the final per-position nucleotide distribution. Training optimizes a weighted sum of gate, PWM (L1 + IC + entropy), and MoE auxiliary losses.
