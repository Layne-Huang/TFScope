\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Predicting the DNA sequence preference of a transcription factor (TF) from its
protein sequence alone remains a central problem in gene-regulatory genomics.
State-of-the-art accuracy is achieved by structure-based models that require an
experimentally determined or predicted protein--DNA complex, limiting their use
for the large fraction of TFs and designed proteins that lack a reliable
structure. Here we introduce \textbf{TFScope}, a sequence-only model that maps a
TF DNA-binding domain (DBD) to a position weight matrix (PWM) using a
\emph{frozen} protein language model (ESM-2 650M) adapted with low-rank
(LoRA) fine-tuning, a residue mixture-of-experts, and a contact-aware motif head.
On a leakage-controlled benchmark in which held-out TFs share no training
identity, a five-seed TFScope ensemble reaches a motif content correlation of
$0.664$ and is statistically indistinguishable from the structure-based method
DeepPBS (mean gate-oracle $r$ $0.657$ vs $0.626$; bootstrap $p=0.30$), despite
using no structure at inference. An independent structure predictor (Boltz-2)
judges TFScope's sequence-only consensus motifs to form protein--DNA complexes at
least as confidently as DeepPBS's ($\text{ipTM}\ 0.957$ vs $0.923$; $32/41$ TFs;
Wilcoxon $p=1\times10^{-5}$). Probing the frozen backbone shows that it linearly
encodes DNA-contact residues (AUROC $0.95$) and organizes DBD families
(k-NN purity $0.83$), explaining where the signal comes from; a variance
decomposition attributes $59\%$ of TFScope's edge to genuine within-family
specificity rather than family lookup. We further show directional
single-mutation motif switching (MyoD1 E-box) and application to \emph{de novo}
designed DBDs, and we transparently characterize the model's principal
limitation --- insensitivity to specificity-altering point mutations inherited
from the frozen backbone. TFScope makes accurate specificity prediction available
wherever only a protein sequence is known.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Transcription factors read the genome by binding short DNA motifs, and a compact
summary of that preference --- the position weight matrix (PWM) --- underlies
much of regulatory-genomic analysis, from motif enrichment to variant
interpretation\cite{weirauch2014cisbp,castromondragon2022jaspar}. Experimental
determination of PWMs by protein-binding microarrays or HT-SELEX is
laborious\cite{jolma2013dna}, and only a minority of the estimated $\sim$1,600
human TFs, and essentially none of the rapidly growing space of designed
DNA-binding proteins, have measured specificities. A predictive map from protein
sequence to PWM would close this gap.

Two families of computational approaches exist. Structure-based models, most
recently the geometric deep-learning method DeepPBS\cite{mitra2024deeppbs},
achieve strong accuracy by reading the physicochemical readout of an
experimentally solved or predicted protein--DNA complex. Their accuracy is
gated on structure availability and quality; for TFs without a co-crystal, and
for designed proteins, a structure must first be predicted, adding cost and a
second source of error. Sequence-only models sidestep structure but have
historically lagged, and reported margins are frequently inflated by
\emph{TF leakage} --- test proteins whose close homologs appear in
training\cite{mitra2024deeppbs}.

Protein language models (PLMs) trained on evolutionary-scale sequence data have
transformed sequence-only inference of protein structure and
function\cite{rives2021esm1b,lin2023esm2}. We hypothesized that a frozen PLM
already contains the information needed to specify DNA-binding preference, and
that a lightweight, structure-free adapter could extract it. Here we test this
hypothesis with \textbf{TFScope} and show, under strict leakage control, that a
sequence-only model matches a structure-based one --- reaching accuracy parity
without ever seeing a structure, while remaining applicable to any protein
sequence.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{The TFScope model}

TFScope maps a TF DNA-binding-domain (DBD) protein sequence to a $4\times L$ PWM
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}\TODO{architecture schematic — Fig 1a/b not yet drawn}).
The backbone is a \emph{frozen} ESM-2 650M encoder\cite{lin2023esm2} whose
residue features are a weighted average of the last four layers concatenated with
a DBD indicator; all adaptation is confined to (i) low-rank
LoRA updates\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16), (ii) a token-level residue
mixture-of-experts (2 shared $+$ 8 routed experts) conditioned on a family
embedding, with dual gated-attention pooling (global and DBD-restricted),
(iii) a position gate head whose projection head emits a soft motif gate and a
protein representation, and (iv) a contact-aware PWM head that predicts a prior
PWM and adds a contact-aware correction (Final PWM $=$ Prior $+$ Correction),
where the correction is trained with recognition-residue contact supervision
(train-only). The model is trained on curated protein$\rightarrow$PWM
pairs (train split \texttt{tf\_pwm\_training\_v23}) using DBD-cropped inputs; at
inference it requires only the DBD sequence. We report a five-seed ensemble
(seeds 42/1/7/13/23) whose members are register-aligned (offset $+$
reverse-complement) to a reference frame before averaging. Full architecture and
training details are in Methods.

\subsection*{Sequence-only prediction matches DeepPBS under leakage control}

We evaluated TFScope against the structure-based method
DeepPBS\cite{mitra2024deeppbs} on a shared held-out benchmark
(Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}). Because naive test/train splits over-report accuracy
when homologous TFs leak across the split, we adopted two controls: (i) a
40\%-DBD-identity clustered holdout, and (ii) explicit removal of the $20$
benchmark genes from DeepPBS's training for a gene-disjoint retrain. All PWMs are
compared with a register-invariant, oracle-aligned motif-content correlation
(offset $\pm10$, reverse-complement), the fair metric for methods that do not
share a fixed coordinate frame.

Under the clustered-holdout benchmark ($n{=}84$ structures, $41$ TFs), the
five-seed TFScope ensemble reaches a motif content correlation of
$\mathbf{0.664}$, improving on the single-seed model ($0.629$) and every
component in the ablation ladder (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}\TODO{ladder panel});
by the mean gate-oracle-$r$ protocol TFScope scores $0.657$ versus DeepPBS's
$0.626$ (paired difference $+0.031$, bootstrap $p=0.30$) --- a statistical tie in
TFScope's favor. On the gene-disjoint $20$-gene comparison, a DeepPBS ensemble
retrained to exclude the test genes scores $0.720$ versus TFScope's $0.685$
(paired $\Delta = +0.034$ with a confidence interval crossing zero; the widely
cited pretrained DeepPBS value of $0.806$ is invalid here because those
checkpoints saw all $20$ test genes). We emphasize that TFScope attains this
parity \emph{without structure}. Per-family accuracy is broken down in
Fig.~\ref{fig:perfamily}, and representative predicted logos in
Fig.~\ref{fig:logos}.

\subsection*{An independent structure predictor judges sequence-only motifs as
more foldable}

To obtain a model-independent quality signal, we asked whether the DNA a model
predicts actually forms a confident complex with its TF. For each of $41$ TFs we
folded the protein with its TFScope-ensemble consensus DNA and, separately, with
its DeepPBS consensus DNA, using Boltz-2\cite{wohlwend2024boltz} with
ColabFold\cite{mirdita2022colabfold} multiple-sequence alignments, and compared
the interface confidence (ipTM) of the two complexes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:foldability}). TFScope's sequence-only motifs fold at least as
confidently as DeepPBS's structure-derived motifs (mean ipTM $\mathbf{0.957}$ vs
$0.923$; mean $\Delta = +0.035$; TFScope favored in $\mathbf{32/41}$ TFs;
Wilcoxon $p=1\times10^{-5}$; complex pLDDT $0.948$ vs $0.921$). A length control
confirms the effect is not an artifact of motif length (Spearman
$\rho=-0.43$ between $\Delta$ipTM and $\Delta$motif-length). Because Boltz-2 is
independent of both predictors, this provides orthogonal evidence that TFScope's
sequence-only readout is biophysically coherent.

\subsection*{The frozen backbone encodes DNA-contact residues and family identity}

Why does a sequence-only model reach structure-level accuracy? We probed the
frozen ESM-2 backbone directly. A linear probe on frozen layer-33 residue
embeddings predicts which DBD residues contact DNA (within \SI{4.5}{\angstrom}
in co-crystals) with pooled AUROC $\mathbf{0.95}$ and AUPRC $0.78$ across
$2{,}324$ complexes ($240{,}072$ residues; GroupKFold with no TF shared across
folds; Fig.~\ref{fig:recognition}). Rendered onto a homeodomain--DNA co-crystal
(PBX1, PDB 1B72), the residues TFScope recognizes as DNA-contacting coincide with
the recognition-helix positions that insert into the major groove
(Fig.~\ref{fig:recognition}). Independently, frozen DBD embeddings organize TF
families (k-NN purity $0.83$; linear family-probe accuracy $0.96$),
\TODO{cite ESM DBD family diagnostic figure}. This structural and taxonomic
information is present \emph{before} any TFScope training, and the contact-aware
head is what converts it into a calibrated motif.

\subsection*{TFScope reads within-family specificity, not just family identity}

A model could reach high aggregate accuracy merely by returning a family-average
motif. To test this, we decomposed TFScope's advantage over a random real-PWM
baseline into a family-identity component and a within-family component on the
clustered holdout. We find that $41\%$ of the edge is explained by family
identity but $\mathbf{59\%}$ reflects genuine within-family specificity
(permutation $p=2\times10^{-4}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:mutagenesis}\TODO{decomposition
panel}), showing that TFScope resolves clade-level differences, not only family
lookup.

\subsection*{Case studies: motif switching and designed proteins}

Single amino-acid substitutions in a DBD can rewire specificity. Using a
directional switch score, TFScope reproduces the MyoD1 E-box switch induced by
the L122R substitution ($\Delta_{\mathrm{switch}} = +9.17$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:switch}), and a structure-based ensemble median agrees on the
preferred site (\texttt{CACGTG}). Applied to \emph{de novo} designed DBDs, TFScope
recovers the intended CAC-type preference to a graded degree (strongest for DBP6,
$r=0.65$; Fig.~\ref{fig:dbp}), demonstrating utility beyond natural proteins.

\subsection*{Limitations: insensitivity to specificity-altering point mutations}

We characterize the model's principal failure mode transparently. On the
Barrera\cite{barrera2016survey} panel of homeodomain wild-type/mutant pairs,
TFScope is largely mutation-\emph{blind}: it predicts a mean absolute motif change
of $0.005$ against an observed $0.180$, with near-zero correlation to the
measured effect. This limitation is inherited from the frozen backbone, whose
embeddings change little under single substitutions; it coexists with the
successful \emph{directional} switch score above because the latter reads a
coarse preference shift rather than a fine magnitude. Overcoming point-mutation
sensitivity without sacrificing the model's structure-free generality is the main
open problem (Discussion).

\section*{Discussion}

TFScope shows that accurate TF specificity prediction does not require a
protein--DNA structure: a frozen protein language model already carries the
recognition signal, and a small structure-free adapter suffices to read it out to
motif-level accuracy that matches a strong structure-based method under strict
leakage control. Two aspects make the parity credible rather than a metric
artifact. First, we control TF leakage explicitly --- the dominant reason
sequence-only/structure-based comparisons have been unreliable --- and still
observe parity. Second, an independent structure predictor, blind to both models,
finds TFScope's sequence-only motifs to be at least as foldable as DeepPBS's.

The interpretability analyses locate the signal: the frozen backbone linearly
encodes DNA-contact residues and family identity, and TFScope's advantage is
majority within-family, not family lookup. This reframes what a PLM ``knows''
about DNA binding and suggests the backbone, not the adapter, is the ceiling.
Consistent with that, swapping the motif head for a biophysical
recognition-energy decoder did not help ($0.554 < 0.629$), and swapping the
backbone to a weaker encoder hurt --- the informative bottleneck is the frozen
representation itself.

The clearest limitation, mutation-blindness, follows directly: single
substitutions barely move frozen embeddings, so fine specificity changes are
under-read even though family- and clade-level preferences are captured. Progress
likely requires backbone signals that are sensitive to individual residues ---
for example, contact- or structure-conditioned features --- without reintroducing
a hard structure dependency at inference. \PLACEHOLDER{position relative to
concurrent PLM-based specificity work once literature search (Phase 1) is
complete}

For practitioners, TFScope provides specificity predictions wherever a DBD
sequence is available --- including the majority of TFs without co-crystals and
the growing space of designed DNA-binding proteins --- at the cost of a single
forward pass and no structure.

\section*{Methods}
\small

\subsection*{Model architecture}
Frozen ESM-2 650M (\texttt{esm2\_t33\_650M\_UR50D}) backbone; per-residue features
are a learned weighted average of the last four transformer layers, concatenated
with a binary DBD indicator. Adaptation: LoRA adapters (rank 16); a token-level
residue mixture-of-experts with 2 shared and 8 routed experts, conditioned on a
family embedding; dual gated-attention pooling (a global pool and a
DBD-restricted pool); a position gate head with a projection head emitting a soft
motif gate (up to 20 positions) and a pooled protein representation; and a
contact-aware PWM head producing a prior PWM plus a contact-aware correction
(Final PWM $=$ Prior $+$ Correction). The correction is supervised with
recognition-residue DNA-contact labels during training only; at inference the
model is sequence-only. Output is a $4\times L$ PWM with the gate.

Five seeds (42/1/7/13/23). Each member commits its own motif core via the span

\subsection*{Foldability assay}
For each of $41$ TFs, the protein was folded with (a) its TFScope-ensemble
consensus dsDNA and (b) its DeepPBS consensus dsDNA using

complex pLDDT were read from Boltz confidence outputs. Length control: Spearman
correlation of $\Delta$ipTM with $\Delta$motif-length.

(\SI{4.5}{\angstrom} cutoff from co-crystals), GroupKFold by protein sequence,
$2{,}324$ complexes. Family organization: k-NN purity and linear family probe on
frozen DBD embeddings. PyMOL renders write the contact-probe score into the
B-factor column of PDB 1B72.
